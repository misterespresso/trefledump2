Semantic Microscope

A sentence embedding where every dimension is a sentence. Each of the 448 most-downloaded Python packages was shown once to TypeSafe’s Jev, a probabilistic classifier that answers in calibrated probabilities rather than text, carrying 40 yes/no questions — “It claims to be fast”, “It defines itself against an existing alternative”, “Explaining it to a non-programmer would be hard” — and the answers are the coordinates. Nothing but the authors' one-line summary went in. Topics, maturity and download rank were held back so they could be used as held-out truth.

The honest headline

The lenses tie TF-IDF (0.727 against 0.737) and win on two targets of seven. Forty readable numbers carry about as much signal as thousands of opaque ones — that is the result, not a victory.

One dead lens

for_developers answered 0.93 with a standard deviation of 0.04. Everything on PyPI is for developers, so the question never discriminates. A lens that agrees with everything measures nothing.

Redundancy is visible

is_library and is_cli correlate at −0.86: the instrument found the library/tool distinction twice. In an opaque embedding that duplication is invisible; here you can read it and delete one.
